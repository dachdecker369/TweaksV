# Roadmap

What I plan for TweaksV next, before it is planned in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) and specified in [SPECIFICATIONS.md](SPECIFICATIONS.md). This file is new with 2.0.0; nothing stood here before 2.1.0, because TweaksV did what I wanted it to do. It held two versions once, 2.2.0 and 2.2.1, both released on 2026-10-25 from a player's report - with NaturalVision Enhanced installed, TweaksV crashed the game; the findings and the design are in NOTES.md and SPECIFICATIONS.md now. Since 2026-10-26 it held six: a limit of the DirectStorage part to lift, not urgent, and five new parts, one adjuster each, towards TweaksV as the one place for tweaking GTA V Enhanced and adjusting its limits; the first of the new parts, Blips, came with 2.3.0 on 2026-10-28, and the DirectStorage limit was lifted with 2.4.0 (NOTES.md, Versions), so it holds four now. Each new part is a part like the nine (SPECIFICATIONS.md, section 16): its own file on the shared code - the scanner, the patch module, the relocation core, the three tables - its own section of `TweaksV.ini`, its old plugin in the conflict check, its measurement under `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE`, its suite, its patterns in PATTERNS.md. One minor version per part, a patch version for its fixes; the numbers below are the order I intend, and a version takes the next free number when it ships - a part that is ready before 2.6.0 takes 2.6.0, as Blips took 2.3.0; 2.5.0 measures the four parts' seven limits first (NOTES.md, Versions). Planned on 2026-10-30 (PLAN.md, D10 to D18), from 2.5.0's numbers and my second test's: WeaponLimits, PedProps and the texture budget ship together as 2.6.0, with two parts that came up in the test - the Static Bounds Limit Adjuster rewritten as TweaksV's own and a check for Legacy packs in RageOpenV - while the streaming heap, which never ran short, is dropped; the ray tracing buffer of moving geometry is researched beside them; SirenSettings follows as 2.7.0.

How a part is taken over depends on the original's license, as [CREDITS.md](CREDITS.md) says of the nine. Code under a license the GPL-3.0 takes in - MIT, BSD, zlib, the GPL-3.0 itself - may be read and adapted: the patterns and the research are credited in CREDITS.md, every line carried over is named in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md, and the part is still built on TweaksV's shared code, as 2.0.0 rebuilt the five plugins, because a plugin merged as it is brings its own scanner, INI and log, what 1.x had. Code without a license, under all rights reserved or under FiveM's terms is idea only: none of it is read, and every game fact behind the part is found anew in the running Enhanced game with my tooling (a test build's dump, Ghidra; PATTERNS.md, "When Game Updates"). The four originals left are Legacy plugins, and no pattern of a Legacy plugin carries over to Enhanced anyway (PATTERNS.md). So every part starts with a research pass in the dump that settles whether the limit is in Enhanced 1.0.1158.16 at all, where it lies, and whether it still binds - Enhanced may have raised it - and the part goes in only when a mod load hits it; a measurement of the limit under `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE`, as the box streamer and the decals have, comes first and says so.

## 2.6.0: WeaponLimits - more weapons.meta files and more weapon components

The original: [Weapon Limits Adjuster](https://www.gta5-mods.com/tools/cweaponinfoblob-limit-adjuster) 2.2 by alexguirre, 2018 to 2022-12-19, once named CWeaponInfoBlob Limit Adjuster, game build 2802 the last it supports; the source is at https://github.com/alexguirre/gtav-WeaponLimitsAdjuster. The game has a hard limit on the number of `weapons.meta` files it loads (`CWeaponInfoBlob`, the plugin's INI default 512) and on the weapon components of all `weaponcomponents.meta` files together (`CWeaponComponentInfo`, default 1024, which since its 2.1 also replaces the pool size `gameconfig.xml` gives); past either the game crashes while loading, and add-on weapons are what reaches it. Its license, checked 2026-10-26: the MIT license (2021, alexguirre) for the plugin's own code, the `CWeaponInfoBlob` half, which may be read and adapted with credit; and FiveM's terms for the function that raises the components limit, adapted from FiveM's `PatchWeaponLimits.cpp`, which FiveM's license does not free - its LGPL list names `code/components/citizen-*`, `font-renderer`, `vfs-core` and the client's shared, core, launcher and common folders, not the game components - so that half is idea only. The plugin's files for the conflict check: `WeaponLimitsAdjuster.asi` and the older `CWeaponInfoBlob Adjuster.asi`. Research: both limits in the Enhanced dump - the blob array and the sites that carry its limit (the relocation core's shape, if it is a static array sized in the code), the components pool and the call that reads its size from the configuration (the PackfileLimit part's shape, a redirected call that replaces a count); whether Enhanced's own limits are higher than Legacy's; what a weapon pack needs. Second of the five: add-on weapons are common on Enhanced, and half of the original may be read.

The research for the weapon infos (2026-10-29, the dump of 1.0.1158.16): the limit is in Enhanced and is 160 `weapons.meta` files - data files of the type 0x4E, one weapon info blob of 248 bytes each - higher than Legacy's 90 at v1365 (the original's page). Not the relocation core's shape: the blobs are one block on the game's heap, which a C++ static initializer allocates with two immediates, the number allocated and the array's capacity (`W-E-I1`), to be raised together in `DllMain`, before the initializer runs, as HeapAdjuster raises its one; the loader (`W-E-I2`) appends a blob per file without a check, so that a file past the capacity damages the game's heap. A load needs one blob per `weapons.meta` file - as a rule one per add-on weapon packed on its own, fewer when a pack merges them; vanilla's own count is not in the dump; my measurement boot of 2.5.0 gave it, 155. Built for 2.6.0 as the pass proposed it (PLAN.md, D12): `[WEAPON_LIMITS] WEAPON_INFO_FILES` from the game's own 160 to 16,384, default 1,024 (254 KB of the game's heap), the two immediates raised in `DllMain`, and beyond the original a guard on the loader that refuses a file past the room and says so in the part's box. Its open risk - what the game does with the rest of a refused pack, and with the 0 the guard returns for a refused file - is my boot of the guards' question: the test build with `TWEAKSV_TEST_GUARD_ROOM=1`, which gives the guard the game's own room, and the seven weapon packs (PLAN.md, After phase B). The idea is the original's MIT half, `CWeaponInfoBlob Adjuster/dllmain.cpp` at its revision 698f729, the only file of its code read; no line of it is carried over.

The weapon components, researched in the dump of 1.0.1158.16 on 2026-10-29 (PATTERNS.md, "WeaponLimits"): the limit is there, the game's own 470 in three places - the list of every definition of all `weaponcomponents.meta` files, a static array of 470 pointers with its count right after it, which the game rebuilds after each file without a bound, so the 471st definition corrupts its memory while it loads; the definitions' pool, 470 slots unless `gameconfig.xml` names a size, which the game's own does not, whose trap ends the game at the 471st first; and the 470 one file may hold. No instruction carries the list's size - it is the distance to its count - and 17 leas load it, 6 of them in the game's second, protected `.text`. The part: the list on the relocation core, with a new kind of limit, the size as the distance to the count that follows the array; the pool given the room and 470 through its redirected size call, so that the pool's trap always comes before the list could overflow; the 470 per file left. The key's name, range and default and the guard were decided on 2026-10-30 (PLAN.md, D12): `WEAPON_COMPONENTS_SIZE` under `[WEAPON_LIMITS]`, 470 to 16384, 1024, and a file refused once the room is used up, with the part's box; the 6 loads in the protected `.text` need the writing half's boot with more than 470 definitions, played a while. Built for 2.6.0 (PLAN.md): the list on the core with its new kind of limit, the follower; the pool given the room and 470 through its redirected size call; the measurement the part's, with the 17 loads read back at every reading; the guard on the loader of one `weaponcomponents.meta`, from the loader research's findings, the pool the room and 470.

Measured with 2.5.0 and decided on 2026-10-30 (PLAN.md, D10 and D12): the base game alone uses 155 of the 160 `weapons.meta` files and 465 of the 470 component definitions, so a weapon pack with more than five of either does not fit, and my weapon boot - seven add-on packs, 11 files and 91 definitions - ended the game in the full component pool's trap 23 seconds into the load. Both halves ship with 2.6.0: `WEAPON_INFO_FILES` and `WEAPON_COMPONENTS_SIZE` under `[WEAPON_LIMITS]`, 1,024 each by default and at most 16,384, each with a guard that refuses what would pass the room and says so in the part's box.

## 2.6.0: PedProps - more props per anchor of a ped

The original: [PedProp Limit Adjuster](https://www.gta5-mods.com/scripts/pedprop-limit-adjuster) 1.0.3889.0 by Zombieguy, uploaded and kept by William Halverd, 2024-04-06 to 2025-07-11: a ped model may have 255 props - hats, glasses and the like - per anchor, an 8-bit limit in RAGE, and the plugin lifts it to 32 bits, mostly for the MP Freemode peds' clothing packs. It is a single-player port of FiveM's pull request 2421 by Disquse, merged 2024-03-19, one file, `code/components/gta-core-five/src/PatchPedPropsLimit.cpp`: a set of the game's functions clamps 32-bit prop indices to 8 bits, and the patch takes the clamps out. Its license, checked 2026-10-26: all rights reserved, so says its page, and FiveM's terms for the pull request's code, which the LGPL list does not free - idea only. Legacy only, from build 2372 on; its author says it is not for Enhanced, and a player reports that it crashes Enhanced. Research: whether Enhanced clamps the prop index the same way and where - the functions the pull request names by their job, found anew; whether anything in story mode reaches 255 props per anchor at all, since the part goes in only when a mod load does; the plugin's file name for the conflict check. Fourth, because story mode rarely needs it.

Research result (the dump of 1.0.1158.16, 2026-10-29; PATTERNS.md, "PedProps"): the limit is in Enhanced. The game counts an anchor's props over all of a model's variation files in 32 bits, but narrows the total to 8 bits in the check the set-prop native asks and in the helmet code, and a worn prop's index to 8 bits before six lookups of its metadata - eleven `movzx` of 8 bits, which the part widens to 16 (`0F B6` to `0F B7`, one byte each, the bound then 65535 per anchor in all), once the calls around them check out and the game calls the per-anchor count exactly 7 times and the metadata lookup exactly 17. From 256 props on one anchor of a model in all, the set-prop native refuses every prop from (total mod 256) on; one variation file holds 255 per anchor by its own data, which no part changes. Whether a load reaches 256 the dump cannot say - it holds no ped data - so the measurement build counts every ped model's props per anchor; packs the size of EUP's, with hats, helmets and glasses for the freemode models, are the likely case. The part: `[PEDPROPS]` with `ENABLED` alone, on by default, since below 256 the widened instructions give the game's own values; no old plugin in the conflict check - the original is Legacy only, and nothing on Enhanced does its job.

Measured with 2.5.0 and my second test (2026-10-30): the base game alone holds 255 head props on the male freemode model and 254 on the female, so a single add-on hat breaks the male one, and every switch to the male model past 255 crashed the game in my test, with packs made for Legacy and converted ones alike, while 255 switched fine. The part is built for 2.6.0 (PLAN.md, D10), `[PEDPROPS]` with `ENABLED` alone, on by default: the eleven narrowings widened in one write once every site, every call and the complete sets have checked out, a narrowing another mod has widened refusing the job; its measurement kept under `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE`, its line per anchor saying whether the limit is raised. Whether it also ends that crash, which lies outside the code the research read, is its boot's question: the converted clothing packs past 255 on the male freemode model, the switch to it and a hat past (total mod 256) put on (PLAN.md, After phase B).

## 2.6.0: Resources - a larger texture budget

The original: [Resource Adjuster](https://www.gta5-mods.com/tools/resource-adjuster) 1.0 by zombieguy, 2022-12-21, no longer updated by its author: "a collection of patches", some of which FiveM uses, that raise the VRAM budget for textures and assets - the game's own a little over 1.5 GiB at the highest settings, the plugin's INI about 3.5 GiB - and the memory for streaming the map and props, with the system's memory detected, for PCs with 8 GB of VRAM and more; it does not replace the packfile, heap or siren adjusters. Its license, checked 2026-10-26: none, and no source published - all rights reserved, idea only; a "Resource Adjuster Enhanced" by another uploader appeared on 2025-12-25 without a description or source, was reported as not working and is withdrawn. Research first, because this is the one that may not apply at all: Enhanced runs on DirectX 12 with memory management of its own, and whether it still has the budgets the Legacy game had, where they lie, and whether raising them does anything against the texture loss the plugin was made for is open; a measurement of the budgets and their use under `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE` says so before anything is patched. Last of the five.

The texture budget's research (2026-10-29, in the dump of 1.0.1158.16): Enhanced keeps the budget the original raised, as data - the streamed textures and buffers may take one of four sizes of video memory from `gameconfig.xml`'s `ConfigGraphics`, `MaxStreamingMemory_0..3_Base` (1,500, 1,500, 3,000 and 3,000 MB in the game's own file), chosen by the texture quality - 3,000 MB at mine, whatever the card - and their number is held below 98,304 (`MaxStreamingResources`); past either the streaming engine evicts what it cannot place, and an update check evicts ahead of time past a factor of the budget, or of Windows' own budget for the game. On my PC it is the first limit a heavy texture load meets, far below the card's 24 GB. It is measured first, with `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE=1`; if the measurement shows a load reaching it, the raise would redirect the call after the start-up's stores and raise the budgets there, clamped to Windows' budget - the key's name, told apart from system memory, and where the clamp's budget comes from are my questions then. No plugin made for Enhanced is known for the conflict check: the withdrawn port's file name is not known.

The streaming memory, researched in the dump of 1.0.1158.16 on 2026-10-29: the sizes the original raised are still in Enhanced but do not bind - the game's two sizes from its configuration, `PhysicalStreamingBuffer` 252 and `VirtualStreamingBuffer` 1000 MB in its `gameconfig.xml` for PC, count only until the streaming engine's first update, which sets both budgets to the resource heap's size, and nothing counts against them; what binds is the resource heap, at most 32 regions of 256 MB, 8 GB in all, which starts with one region, adds regions as allocations need them and never gives one back. `HEAP_SIZE` stays the heap adjuster's: the game heap is not the resource heap. Nothing in the dump or on my map mods' pages showed a load reaching it, so 2.5.0 measured it first, with `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE=1`, and the one write a part could make - the size of a region, two immediates - waited for a boot that showed all 32 regions with refusals. Both of my tests answered it: 6 to 10 of its 32 regions in every start, nothing refused. So the streaming heap is dropped, and 2.6.0 takes its measurement out with its module, its patterns, its suite and its documents (PLAN.md, D10); PATTERNS.md and SPECIFICATIONS.md as tagged 2.5.0 keep its facts and the measurement's design, should a load ever come near the 32 regions.

The texture budget, measured with 2.5.0: 3,004 of the 3,000 MB on my PC, the update check evicting, and 2,682 in the second test. It ships with 2.6.0 (PLAN.md, D12): `VIDEO_MEMORY_BUDGET` in MB under `[RESOURCES]`, 0 keeping the game's own, at most 16,384 - a documented cap rather than a query of Windows' budget, since the game's own update check evicts against that budget anyway; each of the four `MaxStreamingMemory_N_Base` entries below the key is raised to it, none lowered. Built in phase B, in a track of its own: the call after the start-up's stores redirected, the budgets raised there before the game's function runs, a writer of the budgets besides the start-up's two refusing the raise - which the scanner's new search for stores of any width finds - and the measurement kept as the part's own under `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE`; my boot of 2.6.0 with the key raised shows the raise's line and, with `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE=1`, the budget in use in the summary.

## 2.6.0: RayTracing - a larger ray tracing buffer for moving geometry

What I know from my game: with some mod combinations the ray-traced shadows under cars go missing or flicker, because the buffer for high-poly moving objects is too small. The idea: [Resource Extensions [Enhanced]](https://www.gta5-mods.com/scripts/resource-extensions) 2.4 by Koukol, 2026-08-18 to 2026-10-16, a closed-source plugin for Enhanced 1.0.1158.16 that raises many of the game's capacities - among them, with its key `BvhVertexArena`, "extra capacity for dynamic ray-tracing geometry", which its page says stops the shadow flicker. Its license, checked 2026-10-30: no source, and "Redistribution of this tool is not allowed without my permission" - idea only, its plugin never read. Its file, `ResourceExtensions.asi`, is in the conflict check with 2.6.0 for the parts whose jobs it also does - HeapAdjuster, PackfileLimit, Blips and RayTracing (PLAN.md, D13). Researched in phase B (PLAN.md, D16), with LuminanceV's knowledge of the frame - 300 to 490 BLAS refits a frame of the geometry the GPU animation deforms, before the sun shadows trace their TLAS: the buffer is the `bvh_skinning` effect's global vertex buffer, 4,194,304 vertices of 20 bytes, its size an immediate of its set-up and of the two checks that refuse an object whose vertices no longer fit, which then has no ray traced shadow, reflection or occlusion in that frame. Built in 2.6.0 as the part RayTracing: `[RAYTRACING] DYNAMIC_BVH_VERTICES`, 0 by default - a raised buffer costs every player video memory for the whole session, so my INI raises it for my boot - or 4,194,304 to 26,843,545 vertices, the three immediates raised together before the game makes the buffer; with `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE` a measurement that counts every object the full buffer leaves out. My boot of 2.6.0 shows whether my load passes the game's own buffer; the neighbouring limits of the same frame - the job lists, the BLAS build entries, the rope records - stay as they are unless that boot shows the flicker with no refusal counted.

## 2.7.0: SirenSettings - more sirens per vehicle and siren setting IDs past 255

The original: [SirenSetting Limit Adjuster](https://www.gta5-mods.com/scripts/sirensetting-limit-adjuster) 2.0.3 by cp702, 2020 to 2024-07-15, with a 2.0.4 for Legacy build 3788 in its repository, https://github.com/cpast/SirenSetting_Limit_Remover, pushed 2026-05-25. It raises the sirens a vehicle may have from 20 to 32 (`siren1` to `siren32`, `siren_glass` likewise; the 128 bones per vehicle stay), the siren setting ID of `carcols.meta` from 255 to 65535 (0, 255 and 65535 reserved; above 255 the base game's IDs clash with each other) and the number of unique siren settings, and writes a `SirenSettings.log` of every setting and vehicle loaded and every ID clash; its RagePluginHook integration means nothing on Enhanced, which has no RagePluginHook. Its license, checked 2026-10-26: none - the repository has no license file, and its README asks to link to the official upload instead of redistributing - so all rights reserved and idea only, none of its code read. What its author said of the technique on the CitizenFX forum in 2021: the siren setting ID is widened from 8 to 16 bits by turning a good number of byte references in the game's code into word references - many sites, every one to be found anew. Never ported to Enhanced by its author. Research: whether Enhanced keeps the limits of 255 IDs and 20 sirens, where the ID is stored and compared (the vehicle model's variation data, the array of siren settings), and what a car pack with its own `carcols.meta` reaches first; the plugin's file name for the conflict check, probably `SirenSetting_Limit_Adjuster.asi` after its solution. Third: emergency-vehicle packs for Enhanced bring their own siren settings, and 255 IDs run out with many add-ons.

The siren IDs, researched in the Enhanced dump on 2026-10-29 (the findings in my research tools' folder): Enhanced keeps the limit, at 8 bits on the whole load path - a siren setting's ID is a byte, in the setting and in the vehicle variation that names it, and a table of 256 bytes maps an ID to the setting's position, a byte too, in which 0 means no sirens: 256 IDs and 255 settings a vehicle can use (PATTERNS.md, "Siren IDs"). It binds silently: a pack's setting whose ID is in use is dropped, a clash within one file moves the later setting to the first free ID, and the parser cuts an ID above 255 in a file to its low byte (1534 becomes 254 - not 4, as a forum post has it), so packs that use large IDs clash with each other and with the game's own; a pack meets the IDs early, the 255 settings only with large collections. The plugin's file is `SirenSetting_Limit_Adjuster.asi` (the official 2.0.3 zip's, listed on 2026-10-29), a Legacy plugin, which the conflict check leaves out. The part's design: `[SIRENSETTINGS]` with a switch `LARGE_SIREN_IDS=1`, no size - the ID table moved on the relocation core to a fixed 65,536 entries, the loaders' 11 byte loads and stores of an ID made word ones and their 4 limits raised, the dedupe's loop rewritten, the 3 constructors' defaults, the two parser members' types made words - TweaksV's first write to the game's data - and the base loader's call redirected, so that the new table is filled whenever the game fills its own; the index stays a byte, so 255 settings stay the most. The limit is measured first (SPECIFICATIONS.md, section 11.14.3); its open question, what the game's PSO loader makes of a wider member in its binary files, is settled by the base game's numbers with the part off and on.

The research's result for the sirens per vehicle (2026-10-29, the dump of 1.0.1158.16): the limit is there and binds silently. The game lights 20 sirens, `siren1` to `siren20`, in four layers - a siren setting of 0x5E8 bytes holds 20 lights with their count and name behind them, a vehicle's siren state of 0x188 bytes keeps four arrays of 20, its renderer and six more loops run over 20 lights or bones, and the bones' hierarchy has IDs for `siren1` to `siren20` and their glass alone - and the 128 bones per vehicle stay, a bone's index being a signed byte. Packs make emergency vehicles with 20 sirens or with 32, nothing more; one made for 32 loads, and its lights 21 to 32 stay dark. A raise to 32 would take about 100 sites in three layers, the parser's member data in `.data`, which the dump leaves out, and the bones' table, whose allocation and fill were not found. So the limit is measured first (SPECIFICATIONS.md, section 11.14.4): with `DIAGNOSTIC_MODE=1` the measurement shows whether a mod load carries siren bones past `siren20`, and a raise - `SIRENS_PER_VEHICLE`, 20 to 32, 32 being the top the game's own 32-bit sets allow - is decided only after a boot shows such models, and needs a second research pass first.

My second test (2026-10-30) gave the boot D8 of PLAN.md asked for: three packs' 30- and 32-siren models - the CVPI LSPD pack's, the CHP FPIU and the LVMPD Crown Victoria - with 10 to 12 lights the game never lights, half the CHP FPIU's lightbar dark in the game; the siren IDs had room (33 of 255 settings, none colliding). SirenSettings becomes a phase of its own after 2.6.0, as 2.7.0, its second research pass first (PLAN.md, D14).
